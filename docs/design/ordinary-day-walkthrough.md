# Devotionals — one ordinary day

Design walkthrough, 12 September 2026. This illustrates the [product brief](product-brief.md); interface wording and interactions are proposals, not implemented features. It uses the familiar first-session material to show the journey without introducing an unresearched devotional.

## Before this day: a small amount of orientation

On the first visit, Paulo learns that Devotionals offers a shared daily reading followed by time for personal prayer. He chooses a time he can usually protect and, if useful, a reminder. A brief explanation shows that the guide can be consulted between moments of prayer and that he controls the pace.

He does not need to learn every movement before beginning. Help can be available where it is needed. The final onboarding design remains open.

## 1. Make room

It is an ordinary weekday morning. At the time Paulo chose, a reminder invites him to spend time alone with God. He finds a quiet place and opens today's devotional. If today is a busy morning, he can return at another workable time.

The opening makes today's subject easy to find. There is no setup checklist to repeat. Paulo has his Bible nearby and may use his own background music; built-in music is not assumed by this walkthrough.

## 2. Read and meditate on the shared devotional

Today's illustrative subject is **Bringing our decisions before God**. Everyone receiving this day's devotional encounters the same subject and readings.

The Scripture is Matthew 6:9–13, with attention drawn to the petition about God's will being done. Paulo reads the passage in context and lingers over that petition.

A supporting excerpt is selected from William Branham's *The Prophet Elisha* (54-0723, paragraph 10), where the supplied source discusses daily Bible reading and prayer and considering God before decisions. The exact published excerpt is still to be selected and checked against its source.

Optional original commentary connects the daily subject to a concrete experience: bringing a decision before God while acknowledging our own hopes for its outcome. It offers a way to reflect without resolving the prayer for Paulo.

The devotional closes with an original invitation:

> What decision are you carrying today? Bring before God what you hope will happen and ask for His help to respond according to His will.

Paulo has something specific to pray about. A clear invitation to continue into prayer follows the reading. He may also put the device down and begin immediately.

## 3. Continue into personal prayer

The guide remains available nearby. It uses a familiar sequence, with the day's passage giving the reflective prompts their substance. The following wording illustrates the level of guidance; it is not a new trial assignment.

**Arrive.** Bring your attention to your Father. Tell Him what you are carrying into this time, including the decision the reading brought to mind.

**Worship.** Return to the way Jesus addresses the Father. What does this bring to mind about the One you are approaching? Let that become worship or thanks.

**Respond.** Linger over God's will being done. Where do your own hopes make this difficult to pray? Speak honestly about the situation and ask for help to respond faithfully.

Paulo sets the guide down. Prayer develops in his own words. When that thought feels finished, he briefly looks back to discover the next movement. Nothing advances because a timer expires.

**Pray for someone.** Who else is affected by this decision, or who has come to mind? Bring their needs before God too.

**Wait quietly.** Return to the phrase that stayed with you and make room to listen. Consider any significant thought in light of Scripture; quiet without a distinct impression is also welcome.

If attention drifts, a small reminder is available: **notice, gently return, continue**. A concern may become part of prayer; a practical task can be noted briefly and left for later.

The guide does not require Paulo to repeat the whole reading or answer every question. If the initial invitation opens into sustained prayer, he may remain there. If he wants the next movement, it is easy to find.

## 4. Carry something into the day

When ready, Paulo closes with thanks. A brief prompt invites him to consider whether there is a response to carry into today—a decision to bring before God again, a conversation, or care for someone.

Writing a personal note could be an optional future feature; it is not required to close. There is no score, elapsed-time achievement, or demand to mark every movement complete. Putting the device away can be the natural end of the experience.

## 5. Return tomorrow

Tomorrow brings a new shared devotional and the same recognizable way into prayer. Familiarity makes it easier to navigate; Paulo can continue using reflective help whenever useful.

If he misses a day, his next visit opens the current day's devotional. Earlier readings might eventually be available to revisit, but there is no catch-up obligation. The experience supports resuming at the next workable opportunity.

## What this walkthrough leaves open

- Whether reading and prayer appear on one continuous page or in distinct views.
- How much explanatory help is available on demand, and how the user chooses its level.
- How the common daily reading is selected and how local dates are handled.
- How Sunday, holidays, and preparation for communion or feet washing affect content.
- Which optional audio or music capabilities belong in the first version.

These are the next design and research decisions. The core journey is ready to guide that work: make room, read and meditate, continue into personal prayer, and return.
