## My request:
Here's what I have found and what I think it should be the place where we derive features from:
Both the Bible and William Branham emphasize the importance of consistent prayer, but neither specifies a single, mandatory "amount of time" that applies to everyone. Instead, they provide patterns of frequency, specific recommendations for certain occasions, and the principle of constant communion.
The Bible provides several examples regarding the frequency and attitude of prayer:

\* \*\*Pray Without Ceasing:\*\* The Apostle Paul commanded believers to "pray without ceasing" (1 Thessalonians 5:17).
\* \*\*Three Times a Day:\*\* The prophet Daniel followed a pattern of kneeling and praying to God three times a day.
\* \*\*Morning and Evening:\*\* The Scriptures also mention standing to thank and praise the Lord every morning and evening.
\* \*\*Watch and Pray:\*\* Jesus instructed His disciples to "watch and pray" so they would not enter into temptation, specifically noting that they could not watch with Him for even "one hour" during His time of trial
\### \*\*William Branham’s Recommendations\*\*

William Branham taught that while you can work or sing too much, "there's one thing you'll never be able to overdo: that's pray"[12]. He offered several specific guidelines for prayer time:

\*\*1\\. The Universal Prayer Chain (The 9-12-3 Pattern)\*\* Branham established a "universal prayer" or "prayer chain" around the world. He asked believers to pray every morning at \*\*9:00\*\*, at \*\*12:00 noon\*\*, and at \*\*3:00 in the afternoon\*\*[18]. These times were based on the old sacrificial hours of the Old Testament[30][31].

\*\*2\\. Time Suggestions for Meetings and Victory\*\*

\* \*\*An Hour or Two Daily:\*\* For those wishing to support a revival or meeting, he suggested setting aside "an hour or two every day" to pray for the lost and the sick[32][33].
\* \*\*Half-Hour of Prayer:\*\* On certain occasions, he challenged his congregation to spend at least "a half-hour's prayer" before a specific service to ensure a complete spiritual victory[34][35].

\*\*3\\. Long Prayers in the "Secret Closet"\*\* Branham distinguished between public and private prayer. He taught that long prayers—whether they last \*\*all day, all night, or two hours\*\*—should be done in a "secret closet" with the door closed[36][37]. Public prayers, however, should be kept short and direct to maintain the attention of the people[36][37].

\*\*4\\. Historical Examples of Devotion\*\* He often cited \*\*Susanna Wesley\*\* (mother of John and Charles Wesley) as an example of prayerful devotion. He noted that despite having seventeen children and no modern conveniences, she spent \*\*three hours a day\*\* in prayer[38].

\### \*\*Summary of Frequency\*\*

Branham summarized the Christian's prayer life as a constant necessity, stating that believers should \*\*"read the Bible every day"\*\* and \*\*"pray every day"\*\*[41][42]. He warned that letting down in one's prayer life would cause the "supply of blessings" to be cut off[41][42]. Ultimately, he taught that prayer is talking to God in deepest sincerity, rather than just repeating words, and that believers should pray until they truly enter into the presence of God[43].

## Assistant

The strongest direction I see is **helping someone move from reading God’s Word into personal prayer, with enough guidance to begin and enough freedom to stay there.**

I read Luther’s letter. Three ideas are especially useful: preparing the heart through Scripture, responding through **instruction → thanksgiving → confession → petition**, and setting the guide aside when a particular thought leads into deeper prayer. He also explicitly allows someone to dwell on a single portion instead of completing everything. These appear on printed pages 3, 7, 9–10, and 17. :codex-file-citation{path="/Users/paulo/Desktop/A-Simple-Way-to-Pray-Martin-Luther.pdf" purpose="source"}

We’ll exclude the Apostles’ Creed from the devotional framework. We can adapt Luther’s method using biblical passages without adopting his entire commentary.

From that foundation, I would propose these features:

- **A daily invitation to private prayer.** A simple entry into today’s session, with optional morning, evening, or custom reminders. Your researched 9–12–3 pattern could become an optional preset once its sermon references are confirmed.
- **A reading that opens into prayer.** Scripture and a related, clearly attributed sermon excerpt, followed by a few prompts that help the person respond in their own words.
- **Four movements of reflection.** What does this passage teach? What can I thank God for? What should I confess? What help should I ask for? Applying Luther’s approach beyond the commandments would be our adaptation.
- **A guided Lord’s Prayer experience.** Move through its petitions with room for personal concerns, forgiveness, family, and intercession.
- **An open-ended prayer space.** Pause guidance, remain with a passage, or continue in silence. Audio guidance should leave meaningful pauses and advance at the person’s pace.
- **A personal rhythm.** Let people choose time they want to set aside, without treating a duration as a spiritual requirement. Longer prayer remains possible without an automatic ending.

This suggests a first experience: **settle → read → reflect → pray → continue quietly**. Music, ambient sound, and reading plans can support that experience, with silence always available.

One distinction matters before turning your research into published guidance: the numbered Branham citations currently lack their source list. We should preserve those claims as research awaiting verification, especially the specific prayer times and durations. Each published excerpt should carry its sermon title, date, and paragraph or timestamp.

My recommendation is to define and try **one complete devotional session** before expanding the feature list. That will show whether the guidance actually helps you spend time alone with God.
