# Session 1 — first trial feedback

Recorded 2026-09-11 from Paulo's feedback after trying `01-alone-with-god.md`.

## Reported experience

- The guide was a good help and led into roughly 25 minutes of prayer; Paulo described this as a promising start.
- The Lord's Prayer was very familiar, which Paulo felt may have limited further exploration during prayer.
- Paulo consulted the guide throughout the session, stopping reading to dedicate attention to prayer.
- Once the guide was put down, prayer began naturally.
- After some time, Paulo felt he needed background music to stay focused and listened to part of https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hrTH-SxU7sE . The music itself has not been inspected.

## Provisional design implications

Paulo clarified that returning to the guide felt helpful: the previous topic had been expanded and felt finished, and he wanted to discover the next movement without knowing it from memory. Brief reading did not feel interruptive. Some of the explanatory text felt excessive, and a short prompt per movement seemed worth trying.

Paulo felt expected to follow every step, partly because he was deliberately trying the full plan to provide meaningful feedback. This does not establish that the guide alone created completion pressure.

An initial condensed version was proposed, retaining the sequence and Scripture. On reading it, Paulo noted that it resembled an ACTS outline (Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication). This was not an objection to ACTS: the shorter guide explained what to do but offered too little help with exploration, meditation, what to pray about, and allowing Scripture to shape time with God. This is feedback on the draft, not a second completed prayer trial.

The revised experiment pairs each movement with a specific detail from the passage and an open invitation into personal prayer. Reduce procedural explanation while preserving reflective help. The sequence remains useful for finding the next movement when ready; prompt length alone is not the design target. The revised guide has not yet been tested. Music may be used according to preference, and familiarity from repeating the passage may also affect the experience. Audio is not yet indicated as a solution to a demonstrated reading problem.

Music is a preference to explore, and passage familiarity is a hypothesis to test rather than a reason to discard familiar Scripture. One trial does not establish long-term habit formation or a universal session duration.
