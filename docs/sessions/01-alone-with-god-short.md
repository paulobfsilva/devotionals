# Alone with God — a guide for reflection and prayer

Choose a quiet place, bring your Bible, and use background music if helpful. Stay with each movement while prayer unfolds; glance at the next when you are ready. You may finish without visiting every movement.

### Arrive
Read Matthew 6:6 slowly. Notice that Jesus speaks of your Father seeing what happens in secret. What are you carrying today that others may not see? Begin by bringing that honestly before Him.

### Worship
Read Matthew 6:9. Pause over addressing God as Father and honoring His name. What does this bring to mind about the One you are approaching? Let that thought become worship or thanks in your own words.

### Read and respond
Read Matthew 6:9–13 in full, then return to the petition about God's will being done. Where in your life does that petition become personal today? Bring one situation before Him, including what you hope will happen and what feels difficult to entrust to Him.

If you need another opening, linger over daily bread or forgiveness. What need can you acknowledge? Is there forgiveness to seek or extend? Choose the thought that helps you pray and stay with it.

### Pray for someone
Notice how the prayer speaks of “our” and “us.” Bring one person to mind. How might its petitions—for provision, forgiveness, or deliverance—help you pray for what they are facing?

### Wait quietly
Return to the phrase that has stayed with you. Set the guide aside and wait quietly before God, giving room to listen. If a thought arises, consider it in light of the passage; you may also rest here without a distinct impression.

### Close
As you give thanks, consider whether anything you prayed invites a response today: a need to entrust to God, forgiveness to seek, or care to offer someone. Carry that with you when you finish.

**Whenever attention wanders:** notice, gently return, continue.

---

Afterward: Which phrase or question opened into personal prayer? Where did you need more help, or find more words than you needed? Feedback from using only part of the guide is useful too.
